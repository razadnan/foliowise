What do these numbers mean?
Expected return
The estimated average growth per year over a long horizon based on past market data. Real results will vary from year to year.
A rough year
A bad-but-realistic year that happens about once in 20 years (~5% worst outcomes). It shows how much you could temporarily lose, so you're prepared.
Swings (swings)
How much the portfolio value bounces up and down over shorter periods. Calmer swings help investors stay disciplined.

    What our testing showed (2020–2026 backtest)

    We tested these strategies over 6.8 real years of Indian market history (January 2020 to November 2026), including the COVID crash, bull rally, and sideways markets.

    Honest verdict: In our historical testing, a simple equal split earned 16.8% per year, beating both the NIFTY 50 index (11.7%) and our model-guided mix (9.8%). Machine learning predictions generated frequent rebalancing trades (50% monthly turnover), and trading frictions ate into profits. We show this plainly so you can decide based on real evidence rather than false hype.

    How it works

    1. We study past pricesA computer model looks at how large Indian companies have moved over the past decade.
    2. We estimate what may come nextMachine learning estimates which companies look stronger or steadier. These are estimates, not promises.
    3. We split your money to match your comfortA simple scoring rule tilts toward steadier or faster-growing companies based on your risk comfort, capping each company so your money stays spread out.

    Common questions

    Is this financial advice?

    No. This is an educational tool built for a college project. Please talk to a SEBI-registered adviser before investing real money.

    I already own stocks. Do I have to sell?

    No. Choose "I already invest" and tick "Don't sell anything". We'll then show how to use only new money to move closer to a better mix. Selling at a profit can trigger capital gains tax, so check before you sell.

    Why does a higher comfort level mean more risk?

    Companies with higher expected returns usually swing more in price. If you stay calm during drops, we lean toward them. If not, we lean toward steadier companies.

    What does "a rough year" mean?

    It's a bad-but-possible outcome, one that happens about once in 20 years in our estimate. It is not the worst case.

    Are these live prices?

    Not yet. This demo uses sample figures for 27 large companies. A live version would load fresh prices and the model's latest estimates.

    FolioWise is a student project for learning. Past performance does not guarantee future results. Investments in the stock market carry risk, including loss of money.
